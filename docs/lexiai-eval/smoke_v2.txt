### Why would a ship's GPS suddenly show it on land?
[контекст: GNSS Anti-Jamming and Integrity: Physics and maths; GNSS Anti-Jamming and Integrity: Technology and standards; GNSS Anti-Jamming and Integrity: Threats; GNSS Anti-Jamming and Integrity]
This sounds like a case of “noise jamming”. A navigation signal arrives at the Earth's surface at about -130 dBm, roughly 20 dB below the thermal noise in the receiver bandwidth. A transmitter of a few watts tens of kilometres away raises the noise floor enough for the receiver to lose lock. Variants include broadband noise, continuous wave (CW), swept-frequency (chirp) and pulsed jamming. Tell-tale signs: the ship moves but its location doesn’t change.
